| D21 | One home per cost: every recurring cost is a bill; a category's budget is its bills (monthly equivalent of your share) plus what you set aside on top (budget half replaced by D29) | Housing costs were a third place that made a bill and a budget line; migration 0003 folded them into bills. Only the part beyond the bills is stored and used in the forecast, so bills are never counted twice. |

| D22 | PDF statements are read once at upload into a CSV-shaped table (pdfminer.six, positions of characters) and then go through the CSV import | Amounts sit in columns by right edge; header words name them. Scans (no text) are refused. |
| D23 | Casual pay: a schedule marked as varying expects the average received per payday over 13 weeks, from deposits matching its text | Paydays with nothing paid count as zero; the window starts at the first deposit seen. A quarterly pay averages over a year instead. Since D25 the match text is off the form, so a varying schedule expects the typical pay entered; saving it clears any old text. |

| D24 | Square sync: a second outside service, off by default, per user, read only, fixed host (removed by D55) | Payments (gross) and fees as separate rows, refunds, payouts out to the bank, into one account as an import, every six hours with a week of overlap; ids make it idempotent. Unpaid invoices show on the calendar, not in the forecast, which already averages business income. The token is stored like the ntfy token. |

| D25 | Spending is budgeted by hand, not imported | Transactions, import, rules and Square are hidden from the menus; the code and data stay, so they can come back. A person takes spending off a budget as they go and types in their account balances (about weekly). Spent = hand entries plus their share of bills marked paid that fall due in the period. The budget month starts on a chosen day. Every budget replaces the spending average in the forecast. |

| D26 | Payslip PDFs fill in the payslip form; nothing is saved until the person adds it | Read with the statement reader's lines and cells, by label (Total Earnings, PAYG, Net Pay, Payment Date, Paid By and common variants), this pay before year to date. The file is read in memory and not kept. Missing figures and gross less tax not equalling net are pointed out. |

| D27 | Payslips record paydays: a payslip linked to a schedule payday replaces its expected pay in the forecast, calendar, budget and tax; unlinked ones are one-offs | One expansion (`services/pay.items`) for all four. A recorded pay counts on the payslip's date. Paydays in the last eight weeks without one are asked for; "no pay" is a zero payslip, which a varying schedule's average counts. Matching is by date within three days, a schedule named like the employer first; the person can change it before saving. Payslips from before this stay one-offs, and past one-offs are left out of the budget and calendar so they do not double a payday. |
| D28 | A budget line spent every few months is saved up | Every 2, 3 or 6 months or yearly. Its monthly share builds up from the budget month it was set (plus what the person says is already saved), and hand spending and bills paid in the category come out of that; it is over only when more is spent than saved. Changing it carries the saved amount forward. The amount is kept as typed for its cycle, so a cycle saves exactly it ($2,480 a year, not 12 x $206.67). The forecast still spends it a month at a time. A budget import keeps each line's cycle from its frequency (quarterly, six-monthly, yearly); a category takes the longest of its lines. |

| D29 | A category's budget is what is allocated to it; bills are paid out of it, not added to it (replaces the budget half of D21) | Adding a bill used to raise its category's budget. Now a bill shows beside the budget and, once marked paid, comes off it as spent; a saved-up line (D28) saves the allocation, not the allocation plus its bills. Migration 0009 added each person's recurring bills to their stored budgets, so totals were unchanged on upgrade. The forecast still adds only the part of a budget beyond its recurring bills, which are forecast from their dates. |

| D30 | Quarterly pay is spread over the budget months | In the budget, a quarterly schedule counts its expected net x 4 / 12 every budget month from its first payday's month, and its paydays and payslips are not counted again. The calendar, forecast and tax keep the real paydays. |

| D31 | Tax is on income less a typed-in deductions total; no business lines and no LITO | Sole trader income comes in as pay, so the tax page shows income (payslips and expected pays) less one deductions figure per financial year that the person types in and updates. Business categories no longer feed it. The low income tax offset is left out: on a part year it showed an offset the full year would not get. The set-aside target is the projected amount owing at 30 June, saved evenly: the share of it due by today, and what a week is still needed to reach it. |
| D32 | Savings goals live inside real accounts, not as extra accounts | A bank may allow one offset, so an account keeps the one balance the bank shows, and goals split it: what each holds is its moves added up, and the rest is unallocated. A goal with a target and a date shows what to put in each month: what was left to save at the start of the budget month over the months left (rounded up). Goals are not budget lines (D33). A goal kept aside (the default) is left out of "free in accounts". The money never leaves its account, so the forecast and offset interest do not change. An amount already saved when the goal is made is dated before the budget month, so it is not that month's saving. |
| D33 | Goals stay out of the budget; one goal can be the tax money | A goal is somewhere to put extra money, not a monthly cost, so it has no budget line and nothing it holds counts as budgeted or spent. What a goal kept aside holds only comes off "free in accounts". A goal marked `is_tax` (one per person) adds what it holds to the tax page's set-aside figure, unless it sits in a set-aside account whose balance already counts it. |
| D34 | A goal can be saved from a budget line | For money budgeted monthly but paid every few months from a pot. Putting aside from the line records a budget spend in its category and a goal move, linked, in one step: the line counts it used and the goal keeps it out of "free in accounts", so the free figure does not change. Removing either half removes both, and deleting the goal removes them all. Taking money out of the goal (to pay the bill) is never a spend; a bill marked paid in that category still is, so the person pays it from the goal instead. Deleting the category unlinks the goal and keeps what it holds. |
| D35 | Budget lines are grouped and ordered by hand, with arrows | Groups are per person and only lay out the budget page: each shows its lines' budget, spent and left. Up and down buttons swap a line with its neighbour among the budget lines in its group (or a group with its neighbour), not drag and drop, so it works the same on a phone and needs no script. A line never moved keeps the old order (income last, bigger budgets first) after the moved ones; a line put in a group goes to its end. Removing a group keeps its lines, ungrouped. A group folds to its totals (tapping its name, or "Totals only" for all), kept on the server so it stays folded on every device; putting a line in a folded group opens it. Home loan repayments stay at the top, outside the groups. |
| D36 | A goal saves from several budget lines; a saved-up line checks its next bill | The link sits on the line (`categories.goal_id`): a line saves into one goal, a goal takes any number, so yearly bills can share one pot. Put aside spends from the line it is pressed on; on the goal page the person picks the line. A saved-up line with a bill still to pay shows the bill's due date and whether the line will hold it by then: what it holds now, a month's budget for each budget month before the one the bill falls in, and what was put aside from it into a goal since its last bill. Bills still unpaid from before the budget month are checked first and shown as overdue (D40). |
| D37 | The transactions, statement import, rules and payment-matching screens are removed (ends the "code stays" half of D25; D8 and D22 go with the import) | Hidden since D25 and reachable only by typing a URL. Gone: the transactions page and CSV export, CSV and PDF statement import with mapping, preview and undo, the Android share target, rules and recategorising, Match payments and matching a bill by hand. Bills are marked paid by hand. The tables and their rows stay (but for `import_mappings`, which nothing read: migration 0027 dropped it); Square sync wrote transactions, applied rules and matched bills until D55. The categories page keeps categories and their flags. |
| D38 | Each job's expected pay is taxed on its own figures; year to date is a share of the year (updates D31) | A schedule's gross and tax come from the schedule (a gross alone means gross less net was withheld), else from that job's payslips over the last year, else nothing withheld, which the page says: right for contract income and the safe side for setting aside. Borrowing another job's PAYG rate hid a tax bill. Year to date is the income so far with its share of the projected year's deductions and tax; tax on a part year alone gets the whole tax-free threshold and looked like a refund. Paydays this year before today without a payslip are listed (they are in neither column), and ntfy can remind about one the day after; today's is projected, as its pay may not be in yet. A payslip saved as a one-off is extra income, not its payday's, so the page says to choose the payday instead. |
| D39 | Typed-in balances are kept by day | Typing a balance moves the opening balance (D25), which rewrote every past day, so an offset entered today claimed a year of savings. Each typed balance is kept, one a day; a past day's balance is the last one on or before it plus later transactions. Offset savings use it and count from the first one, and say so. Accounts never typed in (transactions only) work as before. Today's balances and the forecast are unchanged. |
| D40 | Overdue bills are shown wherever money is planned | Overdue is unpaid within the last 62 days (`bills.OVERDUE_CARRY_DAYS`), the same window everywhere: the dashboard and bills list (with Mark paid), ntfy, the budget and the forecast, which counts them going out tomorrow. An agenda that reaches today lists those due before its first day; later ones are on their own days. A saved-up line checks them before its next bill. |
| D41 | Loan interest around the balance date | A lender's balance leaves out interest accrued since the last charge, so the first charge after the balance date includes it (on that balance and today's offset). The payoff projection steps from the balance date, so repayments since an old balance count; its interest is what is still to come after today. |
| D42 | A bill marked paid by hand or a payslip moves its account's balance (updates D25, D39) | Each writes one transaction linked to it (`transactions.bill_payment_id`, `payslip_id`), changed when it is and deleted with it: the person's share of the bill on the day it is marked paid, or the payslip's net on its date, in the share's or payslip's account (else the schedule's, else the spending account), as the forecast has them. A typed-in balance counts everything to the end of its day, so a balance is the last one typed in plus the transactions dated after that day, as D39 already worked out past days; one dated on or before it changes nothing, so a payslip put in after a balance that already holds it (a catch-up payday, a bulk upload), a bill marked paid on the day its balance is typed in, or undoing either, never counts twice, and typing a balance in again sets it as before. A bill is dated the day it is marked paid, the `paid_on` the bill shows, so one the bank took before a typed balance but marked paid after it comes off again until the next. A bill matched to a Square payment adds nothing more. They are left out of the spending average and casual pay's deposits. The account page lists what moved it since it was typed in. |
| D43 | What a saved-up line holds is set as the budget shows it, and can be lined up with its next bill (updates D28, D36) | Saved now on a line's form is what it holds this budget month, as Left shows it (the month's share in, what came off it out), and is filled in with that; it saves on from there. It used to be what was saved before the month, so the line showed a month's share more than was typed. A line its next bill would find short offers Set saved to: the bills less the months still to come and what was put aside since the last bill, so it has just enough on the due date. Put-asides count from the last bill even when the line was changed since; an edit carried the line forward but dropped them. What a saved-up line holds is part of Still to spend, so it never counts as free in accounts, and the page says how much. |
| D44 | A saved-up line checks every bill in the year ahead, not only its next (updates D36, D43) | Two bills in one line (two cars' rego on different days) were checked one at a time: the first was on track, and once it was paid the line was short for the second. Each budget month in the twelve from the one shown that holds a bill is checked against everything due up to its end, after any overdue. A bill marked paid before its budget month has not come off the line yet, so it still counts in what later bills need. The line shows its next bill and, when a later one is where it falls furthest short, that one too; Set saved to covers them all. |
| D45 | A typed-in balance has what was put in before it was typed, not all of its day (updates D42) | Pay landing in the afternoon, put in after the morning's balance, moved nothing: the balance held its whole day. When a balance is typed in is now kept (`account_balances.typed_at`), and a payslip, bill marked paid or Square payment dated its day and put in after it moves it, as one dated after it does. One dated before its day, or put in before it was typed, still changes nothing, so a catch-up payday or bulk upload never counts twice; one put in just after a balance that already has it does, and typing the balance in again sets it. Balances typed in before this hold their whole day. Typing in every balance at once saves only the ones changed from what they showed, so an account left as it was keeps moving. A payslip a balance typed in on or after its date already has says so when it is saved. |
| D46 | The forecast chart shows the average and the balance without goals, not dots; a scenario can add income | The dots for paydays and large bills told the person little, so they and the large-bill setting are gone. A dashed line is the combined balance averaged over every day shown, today included. Without goals is the combined balance less what open goals kept aside hold today (`goals.held_outside`, the money the budget leaves out of free in accounts): the money stays in its accounts (D32), so it is the same amount off every day, and future put-asides are not guessed. It has a line on the chart and a column in the month-end table when a goal holds anything. A new recurring income (net, how often, from and to, into an account) is a scenario change, a flow of its own kind, so an income change to every pay schedule leaves it as it is. |
| D47 | The forecast shows a trend, not an average, and the balance after goals is Free to spend (updates D46) | A flat average said nothing about where the balance is heading. The trend is the straight line that best fits the combined balance over every day shown (least squares, worked in fractions and rounded to the cent each day), and the page says how much it rises or falls in an average month. "Without goals" read as the balance with no goals, which would be higher, so the line and column are now Free to spend (after goals): all accounts less what goals keep aside today. |
| D48 | Free to spend and a scenario each have their own trend (updates D47) | Each line the person compares gets the straight line that best fits it, in its own colour: all accounts, Free to spend (the same slope, as goals are held where they are today) and the scenario being compared, whose rise a month the page gives beside the real one. |
| D49 | Spending taken off a budget comes off the account it was paid from (updates D25, D42) | Free in accounts is meant to be what can be spent, but a hand spend took itself off what was still to spend and left the balance as it was until the next one was typed in, so each one raised the free figure by its amount. Now it writes one transaction linked to it (`transactions.budget_spend_id`), as a bill marked paid does: in the account chosen on the form (the spending account by default), dated the spend's day, negative for spending and positive for a refund or income, and deleted with it. "No account" is for cash and writes nothing. A typed balance treats it as D45 says, so a spend the balance already has moves nothing and the page says so. A put-aside into a goal (D34) still writes nothing: the money stays in its account. Spending within a budget now leaves free in accounts as it was; going over takes the overspend off. Spends from before this have no transaction and move nothing; the next balance typed in takes them up. They are left out of the spending average and casual pay's deposits. |
| D50 | Forecast chart lines turned off stay off | Every reload, or going back to the page, drew every line again, so a person who only wanted Free to spend turned the rest off each time. A line turned off or on in the legend is now kept per person (`user_settings.chart_lines`), on the server like folded budget groups (D35), so it holds on every device until turned back. The chart's script sends each click (`POST /forecast/lines`, answered with no content; a failed send, offline say, only loses that click). Keys are the chart's own lines (all accounts, trend, Free to spend and its trend, the scenario and its trend, whichever scenario is compared) and `account:<id>`, the person's own accounts only. A line never clicked shows as before, so accounts past the fourth start hidden unless one is behind the low-balance warning. |
| D51 | The forecast's Free to spend is the budget's Free in accounts now, day by day (updates D46, D47) | The budget took what is still to spend this month off Free in accounts now; the forecast's Free to spend took off only goals, so the two disagreed by exactly that. Free to spend is now the accounts the budget counts (not the tax set-aside account), less what goals keep aside today and what is still budgeted for the rest of each day's budget month (`domain/forecast.still_to_spend`). Today's is the budget page's Still to spend, so the two figures match. Later days spend each line as the forecast does: its share beyond its bills each day, its unpaid bills due that budget month on the day the forecast pays them, and loan repayments on their dates, so spending to budget leaves it level. A line overspent counts nothing. A new budget month gives each line its budget again, on top of what a saved-up line holds, so the line steps down when a budget month starts and up with pay. The legend says Free to spend (no longer "after goals"), and the month-end column and the line's trend use it. Scenarios draw only their balance, as before. |

| D52 | One person keeps Fiscus: no users page, nothing shared, no password (ends D6, D7 and the password half of D4) | The household is one person's to run, so the admin page (users, invites, the audit log view) is gone; backups moved to Settings, the admin's only. Bills are not shared: a bill is an owned row with its own account and category (`bills.user_id`, `account_id`, `category_id`), reached with `get_owned` like any other. Migration 0025 gave each person their share of a shared bill as a bill of their own, with their account, category, payments and reminders sent, so no calendar, budget, forecast or balance moved; a payment left by someone who had left a bill went, its transaction kept. With no admin to turn it on, password sign-in went too (argon2-cffi with it), and migration 0026 dropped its columns. A lost passkey is a recovery link from the CLI, and `cli remove-user` deletes anyone left from before, with everything they own. A bill file's shared_with and split_percent columns are left out. |
| D53 | ntfy to any server, ntfy.sh included (updates D15) | The settings said "your own server", so the public ntfy.sh looked refused, and its own way of writing a topic (`ntfy.sh/mytopic`, or no `https://`) was. Public addresses were always allowed; the form now says so, takes `ntfy.sh` as `https://ntfy.sh` and a topic in the address as the topic, and warns that a topic on ntfy.sh is readable by anyone who knows it. Loopback, link-local and unspecified addresses are still refused. |
| D54 | The calendar opens on the month on every screen (updates D13) | A phone opened on the agenda, which read as a week. The month fits a phone and is what the person expects; the agenda is a tap away. |

| D55 | Square sync is removed (ends D24) | Its page had been out of the menus since D25 while it kept syncing for anyone connected. Gone: the page, the sync job, the client, the invoices on the calendar, and what only it used, the categorisation rules and matching bills to synced payments. Migration 0028 drops its connection (and the access token in it), its invoices, the `rules` table and the bills' matching columns. Transactions it synced, the batches they came in and bill payments matched to them stay: account history points at them. ntfy is the only outside service. |

| D56 | Sessions end with what could have made them | Nothing ended a session but signing out and expiry, so a lost phone still signed in kept the money in view for up to 90 days after its passkey was revoked or a recovery link used. Revoking a passkey now signs out every other session (sessions do not record which passkey made them, and the person revoking is on the one they keep); using a recovery link signs out everywhere before its own; Settings lists where the person is signed in, with "Sign out everywhere else"; `cli sign-out <username>` ends them all. Each is in the audit log. |
| D57 | A paid bill keeps what was paid (updates D25, D29, D42) | The budget counted a paid occurrence at whatever its bill said now and only while the bill ran, so setting next quarter's power estimate, stopping a bill or moving its due date rewrote what earlier months spent, and saved-up lines with them. A payment keeps its amount (`bill_payments.amount_cents`), set when it is marked paid; migration 0029 took each from its transaction, else the synced one it was matched to, else its bill. The budget adds up payments by due date and the bill's category, whatever the bill says now. The calendar and the occurrence panel show what was paid. |
| D58 | Deleting a category keeps what its entries took off accounts (updates D49) | Its spends go with it, and their transactions went with them, so every balance not yet typed in again rose by what was spent in it: money that had left the bank came back. The transactions now stay, unlinked (`dedup_hash` `kept-spend:<id>`, so a spend id SQLite hands out again cannot clash), on the account's list of what moved it, and out of the spending average and casual pay's deposits as the spends were. Removing one spend still takes its transaction with it. |
| D59 | A write takes the lock first and commits before the answer | FastAPI ran a yield dependency's exit after the response was sent, so the commit came after the success redirect: a failed commit (disk full, the database locked) showed "saved" over nothing, and the page after the redirect could read the old data. Routes take the database as `DB`, scoped to the function, so the commit runs first and a failure is the error page. pysqlite begins a transaction at the first write, after the reads, so two quick revokes could both count two passkeys and take both; an unsafe request now begins with `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, and the second waits for the first and counts again. Its body is read first, so one still arriving holds no lock. Reads (GET) and the background jobs are as before. |
| D60 | Passkeys check who holds them (updates D4) | User verification was preferred, so a security key without a PIN signed in on being touched, and the passkey is the only factor (D52). It is now required at registration and sign-in: a face, fingerprint, PIN or screen lock. A key registered without it stops working; another passkey or a recovery link gets back in. |
| D61 | Fiscus is reached through the proxy and needs no secret key | Compose published port 8000 on every interface, so the network reached Fiscus over plain HTTP around the proxy, and traffic Docker's proxy relays arrives from the gateway, inside the trusted proxy range, free to set `X-Forwarded-For`. It is published on `127.0.0.1` by default (`FISCUS_HOST_BIND`), where NPMplus on the same host reaches it; a proxy elsewhere sets `0.0.0.0`. `FISCUS_SECRET_KEY` was required and never read; it is no longer needed, and one left in `.env` is ignored. |
| D62 | Requests are at most 64 MB, and dates run from 1900 to 2199 | A form is read before the route knows who is signed in, so a stranger could send gigabytes into memory or the temporary directory; a body over 64 MB (a file is at most 10 MB) is refused with 413, by its Content-Length or as it streams. Year 9999 crashed the calendar and year 1 a monthly bill, stepping past what Python's dates hold: forms refuse a date outside 1900 to 2199, a URL's falls back to its default, and a payslip date read outside them is no date. |

## 2. Assumptions

- One household, a handful of users. SQLite and an in-process rate limiter are
  sized for that.
- The reverse proxy terminates TLS and sets `X-Forwarded-For` and
  `X-Forwarded-Proto`.
- ANZ exports are headerless `date, amount, description` (a trailing balance
  column is tolerated).

## 3. Open, for later

- Per-account low-balance thresholds (v1 has one per user).
- A per-category view of discretionary spending in the forecast chart.
- An offset-balance forecast in the lifetime savings (v1 holds today's).
- A one-off payslip dated today or earlier is left out of the budget's
  expected pay (D27), so a bonus entered after it lands never shows there.
